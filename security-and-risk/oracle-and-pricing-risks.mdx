---
title: Oracle and Pricing Risks
description: Understand how SOL/USD pricing affects bonding-curve purchases and the risks associated with external price data.
---

RugFreePump uses SOL/USD price data as part of its bonding-curve purchase calculations.

Because this price data comes from an external oracle system, oracle availability, freshness, and correctness are part of the purchase transaction's execution environment.

## Why SOL/USD pricing is used

The RugFreePump bonding curve operates with SOL contributions while the sale's pricing limits and progression are evaluated using USD-denominated values.

The SOL/USD price allows the protocol to translate the SOL amount involved in a purchase into the corresponding USD-denominated value used by the bonding-curve logic.

The oracle therefore affects whether a purchase can be executed and how the purchase is evaluated.

## Oracle data is an external dependency

The RugFreePump program does not independently create the market price of SOL.

It relies on externally produced price data.

This creates several possible failure conditions:

- Price data may become stale.
- The oracle may temporarily become unavailable.
- The reported price may differ from a price displayed by another market or service.
- Network conditions may prevent the transaction from obtaining acceptable oracle data.
- Confidence or verification requirements may cause a transaction to fail.

A failed purchase caused by unacceptable oracle conditions does not necessarily indicate a defect in the bonding curve.

## Stale price data

The program applies freshness requirements to price data used during purchases.

If the supplied price update is outside the protocol's accepted freshness window, the transaction can be rejected.

This protects the bonding-curve calculation from relying on an outdated SOL/USD value.

Users may therefore encounter a failed purchase during an oracle availability or freshness issue even when their wallet has sufficient SOL.

## Price movement

SOL/USD can change between the time a user views a quoted purchase and the time the transaction is processed.

As a result, the final transaction may be evaluated using different price data from what the user initially observed.

Users should treat displayed values as information available at the time they are shown, not as a guaranteed future execution price.

## Oracle price versus token price

The SOL/USD oracle price should not be confused with the price of the token being launched.

The oracle provides information about the value of SOL relative to USD.

The bonding curve determines the token purchase calculation according to the current state of the RugFreePump sale.

After migration, the token's market price is determined by the external liquidity and trading environment rather than by the SOL/USD oracle alone.

## Confidence and verification

The protocol applies validation to the price information supplied to a purchase instruction.

This is intended to prevent clearly unsuitable price data from being used in the transaction.

Oracle validation is nevertheless not a guarantee that the reported market price will exactly match every exchange, market, or user's expected price.

## Oracle outages

If the required price data is unavailable or fails the program's validation requirements, purchases may temporarily fail.

Users should not repeatedly submit transactions solely because a purchase failed due to an oracle-related condition.

Instead, users should inspect the transaction error and wait for the underlying dependency to recover where appropriate.

## Economic risk remains

An accurate and fresh SOL/USD price does not eliminate economic risk.

Users remain exposed to:

- SOL price movement.
- Bonding-curve price movement.
- Token market conditions.
- Slippage.
- Liquidity conditions.
- Changes in the value of the token after purchase.

Oracle correctness and investment outcome are separate concerns.

## External oracle dependency

The oracle is an external component of the transaction environment.

A change, outage, implementation issue, or network problem affecting the oracle provider can affect the ability of RugFreePump purchases to execute.

## User guidance

Before approving a purchase, users should:

1. Review the SOL amount being spent.
2. Review the expected token amount.
3. Review the applicable fee.
4. Review the transaction in their wallet.
5. Consider that SOL/USD and token prices can change before execution.

A transaction should not be considered guaranteed merely because a quote was displayed by the dApp.
